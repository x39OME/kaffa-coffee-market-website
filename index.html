Origin

Kaffa gave coffee its name

Kaffa is a highland region around the town of Bonga. Coffea arabica still grows wild there under the forest canopy, and local families have picked it for centuries. In 2010 UNESCO listed the Kafa forest as a biosphere reserve to protect those wild trees.

Our stall is named for that place. About a third of what we sell comes from Kaffa itself. The rest comes from Yirgacheffe, Guji, Sidama, Limu and Harrar, each with its own character in the cup.

Ethiopian coffee is graded 1 to 5 by defect count and cup quality. We buy Grade 1 and Grade 2 only.

From tree to your door

  1. Picked by hand

    The main harvest runs from October to January. Pickers take only red, ripe cherries and return to each tree several times.

  2. Washed or sun-dried

    Washed lots are pulped and fermented in water. Natural lots dry whole on raised beds for two to four weeks.

  3. Milled and graded

    The dry husk comes off, beans are sorted by size and density, and each lot is cupped and graded.

  4. Roasted in small batches

    We roast on Tuesday and Friday, in batches of 12 kg, and print the roast date on every bag.

  5. Posted the next morning

    Bags leave within 24 hours of roasting. Most coffees taste best 7 to 30 days after the roast date.

The coffee ceremony

Three cups, never one

In Ethiopia coffee is made in front of the guests. Green beans are washed and roasted in a flat pan over coals, and the pan is carried round so everyone can smell the smoke. The beans are pounded in a mortar and boiled in a jebena, a clay pot with a round base and a narrow neck.

Frankincense burns alongside, and popcorn or roasted barley is passed round. The same grounds are brewed three times and poured from a height into small cups called sini. Leaving before the third cup is considered impolite.

  1. First cupAbol

    The strongest round. The eldest guest is served first.

  2. Second cupTona

    Water goes back onto the same grounds. Lighter, and the talk gets going.

  3. Third cupBaraka

    The mildest round. The name means blessing.

We hold a ceremony at the stall every Sunday at 11:00. Pull up a stool.

    Grind size

    The grind for the method you picked is marked. Finer grinds give up their flavour faster, so they go with shorter brews.

    1. Extra fineLike flour · about 100 µmCezve
    2. FineLike caster sugar · about 300 µmEspresso, moka pot, jebena
    3. Medium-fineLike table salt · about 500 µmCone dripper, AeroPress
    4. MediumLike sand · about 700 µmFlat-bottom dripper, batch brewer
    5. Medium-coarseLike rough sand · about 900 µmHourglass brewer
    6. CoarseLike sea salt · about 1,200 µmFrench press, cold brew

    Four numbers that decide the cup

    • 60 g/LRatio

      Start near 60 g of coffee per litre of water, which is 1 : 16 to 1 : 17. The Specialty Coffee Association's reference is 55 g per litre, give or take 10%.

    • 90–96 °CWater temperature

      For filter coffee. Go hotter for light roasts and cooler for dark ones. A kettle left for 30 seconds off the boil is about right.

    • 18–22%Extraction

      The share of the ground coffee that should end up dissolved in the cup. Less tastes sour and thin. More tastes bitter and dry.

    • 150 mg/LMinerals in the water

      Very soft water tastes flat. Very hard water mutes the acidity and scales your kettle. A jug filter gets most tap water close.

    Processing

    How the fruit comes off the bean changes the taste as much as where it grew.

    • Washed

      The skin and pulp are stripped off within hours of picking. The beans ferment in water for 24 to 72 hours to loosen the sticky layer, then are washed clean and dried for one to two weeks.

      Tastes Clean, bright, floral

    • Natural

      Whole cherries are spread on raised beds and turned by hand for two to four weeks until they dry like raisins. The dried fruit is hulled off afterwards.

      Tastes Fruity, sweet, heavy

    • Honey

      The skin comes off but some of the sticky fruit layer stays on while the beans dry. The more fruit left on, the more sweetness and body in the cup.

      Tastes Syrupy, rounded

    • Water decaf

      Green beans are soaked in hot water and the caffeine is filtered out through carbon. No solvents are used, and at least 97% of the caffeine is removed.

      Tastes Softer, less acidity

    Storage and care

    Keeping coffee fresh

    • Keep beans in their bag or an airtight canister, away from light, heat and the oven.
    • Buy whole bean and grind just before you brew. Ground coffee fades within a day or two.
    • Drink it between 7 and 30 days after the roast date. Espresso likes at least 10 days of rest.
    • Keep the bag you're using out of the fridge. It picks up moisture and smells.
    • To keep coffee longer than a month, freeze it in airtight single-brew portions and grind from frozen.

    Cleaning your kit

    • Every brew: rinse the brewer with hot water. Old coffee oil turns rancid.
    • Weekly: wash brewers and servers with unscented soap, and brush out the grinder burrs.
    • Monthly: soak metal filters and press meshes. Boil cloth filters for five minutes.
    • Every 1 to 3 months: descale kettles and machines. Hard water needs it monthly.
    • Jebena: never use soap on unglazed clay. Rinse with hot water and dry it upside down.
    • Moka pot: rinse, dry, and replace the rubber gasket about once a year.

    Glossary

    Arabica
    The species Coffea arabica. Sweeter and more aromatic than robusta, with about half the caffeine.
    Bloom
    The first pour that wets the grounds and lets trapped gas escape. Wait 30 to 45 seconds before pouring on.
    Burr grinder
    Crushes beans between two cutting rings for even particles. Blade grinders chop unevenly.
    Channeling
    Water finding a fast path through an espresso puck, leaving the rest under-extracted.
    Crema
    The tan foam on top of an espresso, made of coffee oils and gas.
    Cupping
    The standard way to taste coffee: coarse grounds steeped in a bowl, tasted from a spoon and scored out of 100.
    Degassing
    Fresh beans release carbon dioxide for days after roasting, which is why bags have a one-way valve.
    Dose
    The weight of dry coffee you brew with.
    Extraction
    How much of the coffee dissolves into the water. The target is 18 to 22%.
    First crack
    The popping sound at about 196 °C when the beans expand and a light roast begins.
    Grade 1
    Ethiopia's top export grade: no more than three defects in a 300 g sample.
    Heirloom
    The catch-all name for Ethiopia's thousands of local arabica varieties.
    Peaberry
    A cherry that grew one round bean instead of two flat ones.
    Ratio
    Coffee to water by weight. 1 : 16 means 1 g of coffee for every 16 g of water.
    Single origin
    Coffee from one farm, washing station or region, not blended.
    Specialty coffee
    Coffee that scores 80 points or more on the 100-point cupping scale.
    Tamping
    Pressing espresso grounds into a level puck so the water passes through evenly.
    TDS
    Total dissolved solids. It measures both how mineral your water is and how strong your brew is.
    Washing station
    The mill where smallholders deliver cherries to be pulped, fermented and dried.
    Yield
    The weight of liquid coffee in the cup. In espresso it is usually twice the dose.

    Questions

    Asked at the counter

    Do you grind the coffee?

    Online orders ship as whole beans because ground coffee goes stale within days. At the stall we grind on request. Tell us which brewer you use.

    When will my order ship?

    We roast on Tuesday and Friday. Orders placed by midnight the day before are roasted that day and posted the next morning. Equipment-only orders ship the next working day.

    How much is shipping?

    $5 flat. Orders of $40 or more ship free.

    How long does coffee stay fresh?

    It tastes best between 7 and 30 days after the roast date printed on the bag. An unopened bag is still good for about three months, only less lively. Once ground, use it within a day or two.

    Which filter fits my dripper?

    Cone 02 papers fit the Cone Dripper 02 and the stainless cone filter. Wave 185 papers fit the Flat-bottom Dripper. The filter size chart covers the rest.

    Does dark roast have more caffeine than light?

    By weight they are about the same. Dark beans are less dense, so a scoop of dark roast holds slightly less coffee. Weigh your dose and the difference disappears.

    How is the decaf made?

    With water and carbon filters, without solvents. At least 97% of the caffeine is removed.

    Can I change or cancel a subscription?

    Yes. Skip a delivery, change the plan or cancel at any time before the roast day by writing to us.

    Can I return something?

    Unused equipment can come back within 30 days. Coffee is roasted to order, so we can't take it back, but if a bag arrives damaged or tastes wrong we replace it.

    Do you sell wholesale?

    Yes, to cafés, shops and offices from 5 kg a month. Write to us and we'll send samples.
