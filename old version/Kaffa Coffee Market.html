Origin

Kaffa gave coffee its name

Kaffa is a highland region around the town of Bonga. Coffea arabica still grows wild there under the forest canopy, and local families have picked it for centuries. In 2010 UNESCO listed the Kafa forest as a biosphere reserve to protect those wild trees.

Our stall is named for that place. About a third of what we sell comes from Kaffa itself. The rest comes from Yirgacheffe, Guji, Sidama, Limu and Harrar, each with its own character in the cup.

Ethiopian coffee is graded 1 to 5 by defect count and cup quality. We buy Grade 1 and Grade 2 only.

From tree to your door

  1. Picked by hand

    The main harvest runs from October to January. Pickers take only red, ripe cherries and return to each tree several times.

  2. Washed or sun-dried

    Washed lots are pulped and fermented in water. Natural lots dry whole on raised beds for two to four weeks.

  3. Milled and graded

    The dry husk comes off, beans are sorted by size and density, and each lot is cupped and graded.

  4. Roasted in small batches

    We roast on Tuesday and Friday, in batches of 12 kg, and print the roast date on every bag.

  5. Posted the next morning

    Bags leave within 24 hours of roasting. Most coffees taste best 7 to 30 days after the roast date.
